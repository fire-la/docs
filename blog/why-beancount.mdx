# Why We Chose Beancount

When building Firela, we evaluated dozens of accounting formats. We chose [Beancount](https://beancount.github.io/docs/). Here's why.

## The Problem with Traditional Finance Apps

Most personal finance tools store your data in proprietary databases:

- **Opaque** - You can't see how your data is structured
- **Locked** - Export formats are limited or non-existent
- **Fragile** - One company decision can orphan years of data

## Enter Plain Text Accounting

Plain text accounting stores your financial records in simple text files:

```beancount
2024-01-15 * "Grocery Store"
  Expenses:Groceries         42.30 USD
  Assets:Bank:Checking       -42.30 USD
```

Benefits:

- **Human-readable** - Anyone can understand it
- **Version-controllable** - Track every change with Git
- **Portable** - Works with any text editor
- **Future-proof** - Text files will always be readable

## Why Beancount Specifically

We evaluated Ledger, hledger, and Beancount. All three are mature, all three enforce balanced entries, and all three support balance assertions — the honest comparison is a matter of fit, not winners:

| | [Beancount](https://beancount.github.io/docs/) | [Ledger](https://ledger-cli.org/doc/ledger.1.html) | [hledger](https://hledger.org/) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unbalanced entries | Rejected outright | Rejected outright | Rejected outright |
| Balance assertions | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Syntax style | Explicit (currency units everywhere) | Terse (amounts elide) | Middle ground |
| Implementation | Python | C++ | Haskell |
| Community | Growing | The original — largest | Active and growing |

We picked Beancount for its explicitness (a feature when your ledger must be auditable for life) and its Python ecosystem. If you prefer terse syntax or come from the Ledger lineage, those are excellent tools too — see [hledger's own fair comparison](https://hledger.org/beancount.html).

## Where Firela Is Going with Beancount

The mobile app is in active development, not yet on app stores. What we're building on top of your plain-text ledger:

1. **Visual editing** - a simple UI for transaction entry
2. **Automated imports** - statements parsed on your device into entries you approve
3. **Reporting** - views over a ledger that already balances

Your underlying `.beancount` files remain pure text, fully under your control.

<Note>
You can always edit your Beancount files directly in any text editor. Firela is a layer on top, not a lock-in.
</Note>
